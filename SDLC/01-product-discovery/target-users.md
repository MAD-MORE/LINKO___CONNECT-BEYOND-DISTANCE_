# Phase 1.3 — Target Users

## Status

**CURRENT — IN PROGRESS**

## Purpose

Identify the people Linko is designed to serve, prioritize the initial market, and define who should and should not be included in the first product release.

## Primary target users

### 1. University students
Students are the primary initial market because they frequently depend on mobile Internet for learning, communication, payments, navigation, and campus activities, while their data availability can change quickly.

Typical need:

> "My data is finished, but a trusted friend still has Internet."

### 2. Friends and family
People who regularly help each other financially or digitally can use Linko to provide temporary connectivity without needing to be physically together.

### 3. Travelers and mobile users
People temporarily outside their normal network environment may benefit when a trusted contact can provide connectivity remotely.

### 4. Users with spare connectivity
Users with suitable data plans or Wi-Fi access may voluntarily become Providers when they have enough capacity to help someone they trust.

## Secondary target users

- Small teams and project groups
- Students collaborating remotely
- Families living in different locations
- Users with multiple SIM cards/devices
- Communities with trusted peer relationships

## Initial market priority

### Market 1 — Ghanaian university students

The initial product should focus on a narrow, testable market rather than attempting worldwide adoption immediately.

Reasons:

- Strong peer-to-peer relationships
- High mobile connectivity dependence
- Clear use cases around students
- Easier controlled beta testing
- Easier collection of qualitative feedback
- Opportunity to establish a local product-market fit before expansion

### Market 2 — Ghanaian consumers

After student validation, expand to friends, families, travelers, and general consumers.

### Market 3 — International users

International expansion follows evidence that the technical model, economics, security, and regulatory approach can support additional markets.

## Provider target profile

A likely Provider:

- Has reliable Internet access.
- Has enough available data/bandwidth to share.
- Trusts the Receiver.
- Wants control over sharing.
- Wants visibility into usage.
- Needs the ability to stop the session immediately.

## Receiver target profile

A likely Receiver:

- Temporarily lacks sufficient Internet access.
- Has a trusted person who can potentially help.
- Wants a simple connection process.
- Needs temporary connectivity rather than a replacement for a permanent Internet plan.
- Accepts that performance depends on the Provider's connection and the Internet path.

## Non-target users for the MVP

The first release should not optimize for:

- Anonymous public Internet sharing
- Uncontrolled hotspot marketplaces
- Users attempting to bypass carrier restrictions
- Users seeking guaranteed unlimited Internet
- High-volume commercial bandwidth resale
- Enterprise networking
- Unknown/untrusted Providers

These may be researched later, but they are outside the initial product scope.

## Jobs to be done

### Receiver

> When I lose or run out of Internet, I want to request connectivity from someone I trust so that I can continue important online activities without needing them to be physically near me.

### Provider

> When someone I trust needs Internet, I want to help them using my available connection while controlling who connects, how much they use, and when the session ends.

## User selection principle

Linko should optimize the MVP around **trusted person-to-person connectivity sharing**, not around maximizing the number of connected strangers.

Trust is a product feature and a security boundary.

## Validation required

The target-user assumptions must be validated through interviews, surveys, prototype testing, and controlled beta usage before major expansion.

We must measure:

- Frequency of losing Internet access
- Willingness to request help
- Willingness to provide connectivity
- Acceptable data limits
- Acceptable session duration
- Trust expectations
- Performance expectations
- Willingness to pay for premium features
