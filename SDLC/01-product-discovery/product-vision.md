# Phase 1.2 — Product Vision

## Status

**CURRENT — IN PROGRESS**

## Vision

**Linko makes trusted Internet sharing possible beyond physical distance.**

A person who has connectivity can voluntarily help a trusted person who needs connectivity by providing an authorized path to the Internet through Linko, subject to the real capabilities and policies of the networks and devices involved.

## Mission

Build a secure, simple, reliable, and economically sustainable Android platform that lets people help each other stay connected when their own Internet access is unavailable or insufficient.

## Product promise

> **Connect Beyond Distance.**

Linko should make the experience feel as simple as asking a trusted friend for help:

**Find → Request → Approve → Connect → Monitor → Disconnect.**

## Long-term vision

Linko should evolve from an Android connectivity-sharing application into a trusted connectivity network where people can:

- Help friends and family remotely.
- Share connectivity under explicit controls.
- Discover trusted people who can help.
- Manage data and time limits.
- Understand the cost and usage of each session.
- Access premium reliability and infrastructure services where appropriate.

The long-term product must remain consent-driven and transparent rather than becoming an uncontrolled Internet-sharing network.

## Product principles

### 1. Trust first
Connectivity sharing is powerful. Users must always know who is providing or receiving access.

### 2. Consent first
A Provider must explicitly authorize a session. Friendship does not automatically grant network access.

### 3. Control first
Providers decide whether to share, for how long, and under what limits. They can terminate a session immediately.

### 4. Security first
The connection architecture must protect sessions against unauthorized access, credential theft, interception, abuse, and privilege escalation.

### 5. Privacy first
Linko should collect the minimum information required to operate the service and should avoid unnecessary retention of browsing contents.

### 6. Reliability
Linko should intelligently handle real-world Internet conditions, including NAT, network switching, packet loss, variable bandwidth, and relay fallback.

### 7. Transparency
Users must understand that Linko consumes the Provider's existing connectivity and that carrier, ISP, device, bandwidth, battery, and geographic limitations still apply.

### 8. Sustainable growth
The product must be designed so that infrastructure and relay costs can be supported by a viable business model.

## Primary product experience

### Receiver

1. Open Linko.
2. Select a trusted friend.
3. Request connectivity.
4. Wait for approval.
5. Connect.
6. See session state and usage.
7. Disconnect when finished.

### Provider

1. Receive a connection request.
2. Verify the requester.
3. Approve or reject.
4. Set available limits where supported.
5. Monitor the active session.
6. Stop the connection at any time.

## Brand direction

**Name:** Linko  
**Tagline:** Connect Beyond Distance

The brand should communicate:

- Connection
- Trust
- Simplicity
- Helpfulness
- Modern networking
- Reliability

## What Linko must never become

Linko must not become a platform that:

- Grants unauthorized access to another person's connection.
- Hides data consumption from Providers.
- Encourages abuse of carrier or ISP services.
- Sells user browsing data.
- Promises impossible network performance.
- Makes security secondary to growth.
- Forces users into monetization before they understand the product.

## Business vision

Linko should eventually generate sustainable revenue through carefully designed premium services while preserving a useful free/core experience.

Potential revenue areas to validate later include:

- Premium reliability features
- Advanced usage controls
- Subscription plans
- Provider/Receiver network services
- Carefully designed infrastructure services
- Partnerships

These are hypotheses, not final business decisions. They must be validated in Phase 18 and Phase 19.

## Success vision

Linko succeeds when users trust it enough to say:

> "I have no data right now, but my friend can help me connect through Linko."

And Providers can confidently say:

> "I know exactly who is using my connection, how much is being used, and I can stop it whenever I want."
