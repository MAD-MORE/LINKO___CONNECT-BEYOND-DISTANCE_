# Phase 1 — Product Discovery

## Objective
Define the problem Linko solves, the target users, the product promise, boundaries, and measurable outcomes before implementation.

## Product vision

**Linko — Connect Beyond Distance** gives people a secure way to voluntarily share Internet connectivity with trusted people regardless of physical distance.

## Problem

Traditional hotspot sharing normally depends on physical proximity. Linko explores a model where an authorized Receiver can route Internet traffic through a Provider's available connection over an Internet tunnel.

## Core roles

### Provider
A person who voluntarily shares an available mobile-data or Wi-Fi connection.

### Receiver
A person who requests and receives authorized connectivity from a Provider.

## Core value proposition

> If someone you trust has Internet access, they can potentially help you connect even when they are far away.

## Target users — initial market

- University students
- Friends and families
- Travelers
- People with multiple SIMs
- Users with large/unlimited data plans
- People who frequently need temporary connectivity

Initial geographic focus: Ghana, followed by controlled international testing.

## Product boundaries

Linko does **not** create free Internet. The Provider's existing connection is consumed by the session, and carrier/network policies still apply.

Linko must never imply that distance, carrier restrictions, bandwidth, latency, battery, NAT, or operating-system limitations disappear.

## Core user journey

1. User creates an account.
2. User registers a device.
3. Users become trusted friends.
4. Receiver selects an available friend.
5. Receiver requests a connection.
6. Provider explicitly approves.
7. Linko establishes a secure tunnel.
8. Receiver uses the authorized connection.
9. Provider can monitor limits and stop sharing.
10. Session ends and usage is recorded.

## Product principles

- Explicit consent
- Security by design
- Privacy by design
- Minimal data collection
- Provider control
- Transparent usage
- Reliable networking
- Sustainable infrastructure economics

## Discovery deliverables

- Product requirements
- User personas
- User journeys
- MVP definition
- Risk register
- Competitive research
- Technical feasibility study
- Monetization hypotheses
- Success metrics

## MVP success criterion

Two authorized Android devices can establish a secure Internet connection where the Receiver's traffic is routed through the Provider, with explicit Provider approval and immediate disconnect capability.

## Exit criteria

Phase 1 is complete when the team agrees on the problem, target market, product scope, MVP success criterion, primary risks, and business hypothesis.
